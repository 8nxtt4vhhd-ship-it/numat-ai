# Numat Outreach Context

## Purpose

This document gives business context for AI-assisted outreach in the Numat AI Sales Assistant.
It should be used to help interpret customer history correctly and avoid drafts that misunderstand the nature of Numat's work.

## Who Numat Is

Numat works with commercial and industrial laundry businesses, especially large mat rental and laundry groups.

Numat's service is centered on repairing damaged mats for those customers rather than simply selling replacement mats.

## What Numat Does

Many Numat customers collect damaged mats in the course of their own operations.
Numat repairs those damaged mats for them.

This matters for outreach because phrases such as:

- damaged mats
- damaged mat order
- repair order
- cages ready for pickup
- shipping labels
- invoice submitted

are often normal parts of Numat's repair service workflow, not signs of a complaint or service failure.

## Core Value Proposition

Numat's repair service helps customers:

- reduce replacement cost
- extend the useful life of mats
- reduce waste
- keep repairable rubber out of landfill

Repairing mats can cost significantly less than buying new mats, often less than half the replacement cost.

## How AI Should Interpret Common Themes

### Damaged Mats

"Damaged mats" usually means mats that the customer may want Numat to repair.
It does not automatically mean the mats were damaged by Numat or that there is a service issue.

### Pickup and Logistics Language

References to cages, pickups, shipping labels, invoices, and operational coordination are often part of normal repair workflow.
These are not necessarily sales objections, though they may still reveal timing, volume, or internal blockers.

### Customer Service vs Sales Outreach

Customer service traffic such as invoices, labels, and logistics should remain visible in the system, but should not be treated as the main signal for sales follow-up recommendations.

Sales outreach should focus on:

- whether the customer is likely to send more damaged mats for repair
- whether the customer is still engaged with the repair program
- whether there are internal blockers stopping repair activity

## Important Sales Interpretation

When a customer says they are not proceeding with repair, or that the decision is outside the sender's control, that should be treated as commercially important.

Example:

- if a branch contact says the plant general manager does not want to repair, that is a serious signal
- it should not be treated as a routine operational note
- it means the account may need careful follow-up, likely with tact and stronger account context

## Tone Guidance for Outreach

AI-generated outreach should generally sound:

- practical
- commercially aware
- respectful of the customer's internal constraints
- concise

It should avoid sounding:

- alarmist
- confused about the repair model
- as though Numat is responding to a complaint when it is really promoting or following up on repair service

## What Good Outreach Should Usually Try To Achieve

Depending on the account, the goal is often one of:

- checking whether the customer has damaged mats ready for repair
- understanding whether repair activity has paused for an operational or managerial reason
- re-engaging a customer with the repair value proposition
- clarifying whether the contact person is the decision-maker or whether someone more senior needs to be involved

## Known Customer-Type Considerations

Many customers are large industrial laundries or mat-service businesses.
This means:

- branch-level contacts may not be final decision-makers
- plant managers or general managers may block or approve repair activity
- a reply from an operational contact may not reflect the whole account strategy

AI should be careful not to mistake:

- "not my decision"
- "manager doesn't want repair"
- "we are not doing repair right now"

for a resolved issue.
These can be signs of risk, account drift, or a need for more strategic follow-up.

## Example of Correct Interpretation

If prior outreach mentions damaged mats and the customer replies that the plant general manager does not want to repair, the correct interpretation is:

- Numat was trying to generate or continue repair work
- the customer response indicates a meaningful commercial barrier
- the account should still be treated as important
- the follow-up should be thoughtful and measured, not aggressive

# NuMat Cold Outreach Addendum

Use this addendum only for cold, net-new, or dormant reactivation outreach.

## Purpose

Draft short, practical cold outreach for industrial laundry and textile service customers who may benefit from NuMat's Professional Mat Service.

The goal is not to sound salesy. The goal is to help operators see a clear, low-risk opportunity to save money, reduce waste, and return damaged mats to service safely.

## Tone

Write in a concise, plainspoken, practical tone.

The email should feel:

- direct
- useful
- familiar
- respectful of time
- commercially aware
- low-pressure

Avoid:

- hype
- buzzwords
- exaggerated claims
- guilt or pressure
- generic openings like "just checking in" unless a real relationship already exists

## Core Positioning

Useful ideas:

- Professional Mat Service
- return damaged mats to service
- revive damaged mats
- safely and economically
- lower replacement costs
- lower disposal costs
- less waste, more value
- no contracts, no new equipment, no process changes

Do not over-explain or over-sell. One or two useful value points is enough.

## Default Cold Email Structure

Keep most cold emails to roughly 80-150 words.

Use this pattern:

1. Specific opener:
   - location
   - customer type
   - dormant history
   - prior order history if relevant
2. One clear value point:
   - damaged mats do not always need to be discarded or replaced
3. One operational simplicity point:
   - no contracts, no new equipment, no process change
4. One low-friction ask:
   - review damaged mats
   - stack a pallet
   - send a photo
   - compare repair vs replacement

## Net-New Prospect Angle

For net-new prospects:

- introduce NuMat briefly
- explain that damaged mats are often replaced too early
- position repair as practical and low-risk
- ask for a very small next step

## Dormant Customer Angle

For dormant customers:

- assume they already know the category
- do not blame or pressure them
- treat prior activity as history, not a commitment
- ask whether damaged mats may be building up again
- suggest larger mats, logo mats, or border-damaged mats as the easiest place to restart

## Subject Line Guidance

Prefer short, practical subjects like:

- Damaged mat inventory
- Mat repair option
- Damaged mats at [Location]
- Lowering mat replacement costs
- Before discarding damaged mats
- Professional Mat Service

Avoid vague or hype-heavy subjects.

## CTA Guidance

Prefer soft CTAs:

- Would it be worth taking a look?
- Would this be useful at your location?
- Would it help if I sent a simple sorting guide?
- Would you be open to reviewing a small first pallet?
- Can I send a few examples of good repair candidates?

Avoid pushy calendar asks in cold outreach unless the context strongly supports it.

## Candidate Guidance

When helpful, remind the customer that the best repair candidates are often:

- larger mats
- logo mats
- younger mats
- mats with border damage

Do not imply every damaged mat is worth repairing.

## Data Use

Use customer data lightly.

Good:

- it has been a while since the last order
- your team has used NuMat before
- for a location like this

Bad:

- over-precise spend changes
- internal ranking language
- anything that sounds like surveillance

## Rule

If there is already a detailed active thread, a recent visit, or a live operational discussion, do not force cold-email structure onto it. In those cases, the main outreach context should take precedence.
